a generation built upon sound

2026 / video art with music

After storms poisoned the earth, people live underground in shelters that never meet, kept alive by generators of sound: organisms that feed on sound, days that are all the same, time measured by a counter. One morning, on the day of the generator’s celebration, a man follows the path behind the machine and climbs out into a cosmic quiet, under an endless sky full of stars. The music is built around a narrated text, given below in English.

Narration

The storms came to our earth many days ago. So many that the old days were lost from our stories. The rain fell and watered the soil with poison. And so everything was lost. Now, here, no one remembers the other life.
Under the ground, a new life began to thrive.
Inside the shelters, the days were different.
And yet the earth is still ours.
Our generation is a generation built upon sound.
From the first shelter to the last, people have never met. We made a new earth here, and we flood it.
The sound generators are always running, and so we go on.
Life dependent on sound.
Organisms that feed on sound.
It reaches our skin like the lava at the centre of our world. And time flows.
The days are the same. But time flows.
The counter on the shelter’s gauge tells how many have passed. How many hours. And so we know that our time flows.
And we know how much sound we have consumed to live, to grow and to reproduce.

Here, light never reaches. Many speak of balls of light nailed to a horizon that never ends. Here everything ends, and the gaze stops at the iron bars close by.
Here the seasons do not change. Here time is a straight line. Here every day is the same. Every place is the same. And we stay still, our steps few and our paths small.
Sound follows us everywhere. And so we live.
That morning, the sound was pounding relentlessly and loudly inside his brain. It echoed in his sternum and struck from one side of his skull to the other.
For a long time now he had been thinking. He listened to conversations at night. And he thought even more.
His body was heavy. In the mornings it melted onto the iron bars and his flesh ran onto the concrete. His diaphragm was weak against the intense frequencies, and behind his eyes, deep inside, nested a pain that had been born with him.
For a long time now he had been thinking about it.
Every day, after the end of the first shift, the loudspeakers said that care is needed. That you must always listen to the sound, so that it does not go out. But for a long time now he had been thinking about it. In his sleep he saw the horizon that never stopped.
They said that in shelter 4 there was a faded crimson door. It had rusted over the years and was dangerous; you could cut your hands just by touching it. Behind the door, in a corridor laid with cloth, stood the generator.
For the generator they held a celebration once every cycle of the counter, and they brought to the shelter’s circle offerings and old things that reminded them of something, though they did not understand what. Inexplicable things that had come into their hands from ancestors now lost in time made a tall heap in the shelter, beneath the counter: an offering to her whose sound feeds the children.
An offering to the machine that keeps life going under the ground.
A different life.
The earth belongs to us.
Today was the celebration. The generator room was empty.
And behind it, a path.
For a long time now he had been thinking about it. The horizon that never ends.
And quiet.
Three days on the path. The sound was fading, yet it could still be heard. His ribs ached.
And on the fourth day, the hatch.
He opened the lid. He walked a little further. Around him, only quiet.
A cosmic quiet that struck his spine.
And yet nothing happened in the quiet.
Before him, a vast, endless sky full of light and stars.

Text translated from the Greek.
